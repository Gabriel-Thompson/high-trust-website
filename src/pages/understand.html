Pillar one

Understand.

Find out what happened, why it happened, and where America stands now.

The first job

Serious analysis without unreadable language.

We need a defensible account of how trust developed in the United States, where it was strongest, what institutions supported it, and which changes weakened it.

The work should use historical records, public data, state comparisons, academic research, government reports, original surveys where useful, and direct interviews with people who operate institutions on the ground.

Plain-language rule: if a finding cannot be explained to an interested voter in a few paragraphs, we have not finished the work.

Research agenda

The questions we want answered.

  1. When and where did American trust rise, and when did it begin to weaken?
  2. Which changes were causes of declining trust, and which were merely symptoms?
  3. Why have some states and communities remained safer, more stable, more competent, or more cohesive than others?
  4. Which institutions still earn public trust, and what do they do differently?
  5. Which policies create accountability, stability, competence, and honest dealing?
  6. Which policies reward disorder, fraud, dependency, incompetence, or institutional failure?
  7. What can elected leaders actually do within one term to improve the conditions that support trust?
Areas of study

What makes daily life trustworthy?

Order & SafetyCan people reasonably expect protection from violence, theft, disorder, and repeat victimization?
Family & CommunityAre families and local institutions stable enough to carry responsibility without constant government substitution?
Honest DealingAre fraud, corruption, cheating, and misuse of systems detected and punished?
Competent GovernmentDo public institutions perform basic duties, spend responsibly, and correct failure?
Work & OpportunityCan ordinary people build a stable life through work, ownership, and enterprise?
Civic LifeDo citizens participate in institutions larger than themselves and smaller than the federal government?
Flagship research

The American High Trust Study

The flagship study will examine the rise and decline of high trust in the United States: establish a timeline, test competing explanations, identify turning points, and separate causes from symptoms.
